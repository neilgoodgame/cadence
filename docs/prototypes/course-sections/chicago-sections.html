How it was computed

  1. Resampled the 850 recorded points (about one every 50 m) onto a 10 m distance grid.
  2. Smoothed with a 30 m running median (removes spikes), then a 100 m running mean.
  3. Segmented into straight lines that stay within 2 m of the smoothed profile everywhere. Bottom-up starts with tiny pieces and repeatedly joins the neighbouring pair that fits a line best. Douglas–Peucker starts with one line and keeps splitting at the worst-fitting point.
  4. Labelled each piece by average grade: steep descent below −4%, descent −4 to −1%, flat ±1% or less, climb 1 to 4%, steep climb 4% or more.
  5. Joined neighbours with the same label, and folded anything shorter than 250 m into the neighbour with the closer grade, unless it rises or drops at least 5 m, so short, sharp hills survive.
